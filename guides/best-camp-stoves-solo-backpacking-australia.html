Best Camping Stoves for Solo Backpacking in Australia

The best lightweight backpacking stoves for solo camping in Australia. Jetboil, MSR, Soto and more compared for weight, boil time and packability.

What matters in a solo backpacking stove

When you're carrying everything on your back, weight and packability are everything. Solo backpacking stoves should weigh under 200g (many under 100g), pack small, and be efficient enough that a 100g gas canister lasts several days of normal use. Boil time matters more than cooking versatility for solo hikers.

Integrated systems vs canister stoves

Integrated systems (Jetboil Flash, MSR WindBurner) include the pot, heat exchanger and stove in one system. They're faster to boil, more fuel-efficient and better in wind, but less versatile for actual cooking — great for boil-in-bag meals and drinks. Lightweight canister stoves (MSR PocketRocket, Soto Windmaster) are more compact but need a separate pot.

Jetboil Flash

The Jetboil Flash is one of the most popular backpacking stove systems in Australia. It boils 500ml in around 100 seconds, packs into itself, and the built-in heat exchanger cuts fuel use significantly. The FluxRing technology makes it notably wind-resistant compared to a basic canister stove.

MSR PocketRocket 2 and WindBurner

MSR's PocketRocket 2 is tiny, cheap and reliable — one of the best-selling lightweight stoves globally. At 73g it packs to almost nothing. The MSR WindBurner is the integrated system competitor to the Jetboil, with excellent wind performance for exposed alpine and coastal conditions in Australia.

Soto Windmaster

The Soto Windmaster is particularly popular with serious backpackers for its exceptional wind resistance without needing a windshield. At 67g and with a small 4Flex pot support, it performs well in the conditions Australian alpine walkers regularly encounter — exposed ridgelines and coastal headlands.

Top picks

Best integrated system: Jetboil Flash. Best ultralight canister: MSR PocketRocket 2. Best wind performance: Soto Windmaster. Best for winter: MSR WindBurner. Best budget: Anatton or generic screw-top canister stove.
