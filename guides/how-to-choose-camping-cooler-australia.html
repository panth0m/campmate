How to Choose the Right Camping Cooler for Australia

A practical guide to choosing a camping cooler in Australia. Covers ice retention, capacity, hard vs soft, and what works in Australian summer heat.

Why cooler choice matters in Australian summer

A cheap cooler might hold ice for 12–24 hours in a mild climate, but in an Australian summer, temperatures regularly exceed 35–40°C in the shade. A quality cooler with proper insulation can mean the difference between cold drinks on day 3 and a box of warm water.

Step 1: How long is your trip?

For day trips or overnighters, a basic $60–$100 icebox is usually fine. For 3–5 day trips, look for coolers rated at 3–5 days ice retention. For longer or more remote trips, a quality roto-moulded cooler (Yeti, Dometic, Canyon Coolers) or a 12V portable fridge is worth the investment.

Step 2: Hard vs soft cooler

Hard coolers last longer and retain ice better. Soft coolers are lighter and fold flat when empty — great for day hikes or beach trips. Portable fridges (12V electric) eliminate ice entirely and give you consistent cold, but need a power source.

Step 3: Capacity

A general guide: 1 litre of cooler capacity per 1kg of food/drink, plus space for ice. A couple on a 3-day trip usually needs 40–50L. A family of 4 camping for a week typically needs 70–100L, or two smaller coolers. Avoid overpacking — ice needs airspace to work.

Step 4: Ice retention

Rotomoulded coolers (thick walls, rubber gaskets) dramatically outperform standard injection-moulded boxes. Pre-chilling your cooler before the trip, using block ice (not cubed) and keeping it in the shade all extend ice life significantly.

Step 5: Portability

A 100L cooler full of food and ice weighs 50kg+. Consider wheeled options for campsite use, or split into two medium coolers. Handles, latches and lid seats (some coolers double as a seat) all add practical value on a real camping trip.
